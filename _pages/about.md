Computational biologist, Ph.D. in Computational Biology from Weill Cornell Medicine.

I completed my PhD in the [Tri-I Computational Biology and Medicine program](https://compbio.triiprograms.org/) at Weill Cornell Medicine and Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, supervised by [Quaid Morris](http://www.morrislab.ai/), and I continue to work in the Morris Lab.

I develop machine-learning methods for RNA biology and translational oncology. My dissertation asked when protein–RNA interactions measured in cells reflect an RNA-binding protein's intrinsic sequence specificity: by reanalyzing 240+ ENCODE eCLIP experiments with an RBP-agnostic CLIP model, I found that only about a third do, with the rest driven by condensate localization, and I developed an intrinsic-specificity score that flags such cases without in vitro data. In collaboration with [Michael Kharas' lab](https://www.mskcc.org/research/ski/labs/michael-kharas), I showed that MSI2 promotes translation of a leukemic stem-cell program in AML through a cooperative RBP module. I also work on protein language models and genomic sequence-to-function models, and I enjoy building AI agents for science (most recently at the Y Combinator Bio × AI Hackathon).

Previously, I completed my B.Sc. and M.Sc. at Antalya Bilim University in Electrical & Electronics Engineering and Computer Engineering under the supervision of [Hilal Kazan](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=vxtfpUQAAAAJ&hl=en).
